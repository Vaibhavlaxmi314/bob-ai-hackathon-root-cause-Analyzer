# Problem Statement

## Background

[This problem belongs to the global logistics and cold chain management industry. Modern supply chains rely on a highly complex, interconnected network of cargo vessels, commercial trucks, and specialized refrigerated containers (called reefers). Because companies want to keep storage costs low, they move goods using precise, tight schedules. At the same time, sensitive goods like vaccines, biologics, and fresh food must stay within strict temperature limits throughout their entire journey to remain safe and usable.]

## The Problem

[Supply chain operations are completely blind to live transit risks and asset bottlenecks. When a sudden disruption occurs—like a sudden storm, a port strike, or a geopolitical crisis—logistics teams cannot instantly see which of their hundreds of active shipments are trapped.Fleet managers cannot easily see where their equipment is underused. While high-priority shipments sit delayed and stranded at a closed border or bottlenecked port, empty trucks, trailers, and containers sit completely idle in nearby regions. Because there is no connection between live route disruptions and available fleet capacity, companies cannot quickly shift their assets to bypass the trouble.]

## Who is Affected

[This problem directly impacts Logistics Operations Managers and Fleet Dispatchers at mid-to-large enterprise shipping companies, manufacturing giants, and retail distributors who coordinate more than 500 active freight shipments across varied global supply chains at any given time.]

## Why It Matters

[1.Skyrocketing Emergency Costs: When forced to rescue a stranded shipment at the last minute, companies must pay spot-market emergency shipping fees that are 2x to 3x higher than standard contract rates.
2.Wasted Assets: Fleet equipment sitting idle wastes thousands of dollars daily. Containers and trucks only make money when they are moving cargo.
3.Production Line Shutdowns: For factories that rely on components arriving right on time, a delayed shipment can cause the entire assembly line to stall. This costs millions of dollars per day in lost productivity.]

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

[1.Data is Fragmented: Current tracking tools operate in isolated silos. Live weather and political risk feeds live in one system, truck locations sit in a legacy Fleet Management System (FMS), and customer orders sit in a separate Transportation Management System (TMS).
2.Manual Problem Solving: When a route is blocked, operators must manually look up alternate paths, call different carriers to check availability, and check spreadsheets to see if they have any idle trucks nearby. This slow, human-driven process takes hours or days—far too slow to keep supply chains moving smoothly.
3.No Predictive Re-Routing: Existing mapping systems only look at where a truck is right now. They do not look ahead to predict how a bottleneck early in the journey will cascade and delay delivery loops later in the week.]
